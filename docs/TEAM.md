# Team ownership and working rhythm

This guide supports the site maintainer's presentation to the current organization leadership. It describes the work needed to complete and maintain the public website; it is not public-facing membership or sponsor material. The internal presentation is maintained in its own repository.

The association is already operating with members, leadership, ongoing activities, and completed projects. The website project does not create that structure; it turns the organization's records into an accurate, maintainable public profile. The website now contains archive-backed draft material for responsible owners to review.

## The central agreement

The technical maintainer publishes the site. **The association owns the content.** Program owners verify facts; the editor assembles the message; someone monitors the participation route.

The public site's direction is **who we are and what we do**. Prioritize an accurate profile, clear project briefs, and real stories. Support and participation are available next steps, not the headline message. Use the same site for outside visitors and residents.

One person may hold several roles in a small team. A role must still have an explicitly agreed primary person and a backup or handover route.

## Responsibility table

| Role | Supplies / does | Checks / decides | Suggested output |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Committee / association lead | Official identity, purpose, geographic scope, participation rules | Public representation and launch priorities | Completed organization brief |
| Content editor | Consistent wording and consolidated updates | Completeness, readability, duplicate/conflicting requests | One ready-to-publish change list |
| Program coordinator | Goal, schedule, location, resources, owner, changes | Accuracy of their activity | One completed program/event brief |
| Photo/documentation contributor | Photos, captions, local stories, sources | Permission and factual context | Small approved asset set |
| Contact / community owner | Monitored destination and response process | Who handles incoming questions and handoffs | Tested contact route and response expectation |
| Technical maintainer | Implementation, checks, deployment, maintenance | Technical correctness and recovery | Working release plus updated handover |
| Technical backup | Access continuity, deployment/recovery familiarity | Ability to obtain help if primary is unavailable | Successfully follow the runbook once |
| Members and residents | Needs, ideas, usability feedback | Whether information is useful and understandable | Specific observations, not just a visual vote |

Fill names and availability in the team's private working space. Public documents can use approved roles instead of personal details.

## From idea to published information

```text
Contributor submits a brief
        ↓
Program owner checks the facts
        ↓
Editor prepares one coherent update
        ↓
Technical maintainer applies and previews
        ↓
Editor + activity owner review the visible result
        ↓
Publish → named owner checks the live page
```

For a simple wording fix, the editor and maintainer can handle the whole update. New organizational commitments, schedules, or participation policies need the responsible owner to confirm them.

## Suggested profile content workshop (30–40 minutes)

1. **2 minutes:** explain that the organization and activities are real, and the current website copy is an archive-backed draft rather than an approved public record.
2. **8–10 minutes:** use the pitch deck; explain that the anniversary motivated the project while the site itself remains an evergreen profile.
3. **8 minutes:** list existing sources for identity, leadership, ongoing activities, completed work, dates, photos, and outcomes.
4. **5 minutes:** choose a small initial activity set, including both ongoing and completed work where possible.
5. **5–10 minutes:** identify gaps, permissions, and the person who can verify each draft.
6. **3 minutes:** name the editor/contact owners and agree the next review date.

Do not resolve every possible future service or rewrite the full organizational history in this meeting. The useful outcome is a small set of sourced drafts with people willing to verify and own them.

## Feedback categories

| Category | Example | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Factual correction | Wrong RW identity or participation rule | Resolve with the responsible owner |
| Missing content | No venue or coordinator for an activity | Request a complete brief |
| Usability problem | A resident cannot find how to help | Observe the path and improve wording/layout |
| Visual preference | A different photo or color feels more local | Compare against the agreed identity |
| Future service idea | Member login or equipment booking | Capture purpose and owner; do not automatically build |

## Proposed release sequence

### Phase 1 — inventory the organization and its record

Collect the official identity, confirmed historical start date, current leadership roles, useful milestones, activity list, existing documentation, intended audience, content editor, and contact owner. Choose whether the voice should stay informal Indonesian, become more formal, or become bilingual.

**Exit condition:** a sourced organization brief, selected activity/history content, and named content/contact owners.

### Phase 2 — prepare and verify real drafts

Prepare a concise organization profile, selected ongoing and completed activities, an optional timeline, approved photos or honest illustrations, participation instructions, and relevant FAQs. Mark the first version as draft, update the site, and review it on phones.

**Exit condition:** every factual claim has a source/owner and review state; no placeholder, unverified date, result, photo, or contact is presented as confirmed.

### Phase 3 — try it with new visitors and residents

Suggested trial: five people, including someone unfamiliar with the association, a potential collaborator, and residents with different levels of digital comfort. Ask each to explain the association, describe an activity and its intended benefit, and find how to make contact. Residents can also try finding local participation information. Observe instead of instructing them.

Record completed tasks, confusing wording, missing information, and the time the contact owner needs to respond to a test inquiry. This is a proposed evaluation method, not an existing result.

**Exit condition:** the core path is understandable and the actual participation route works.

### Phase 4 — launch and establish upkeep

Deploy, add the domain if approved, check the live page, and give the group the final URL. Announce only activities that have been confirmed.

**Exit condition:** working live links, assigned response coverage, a backup with access, and the next content-review date agreed.

## Launch checklist

- [ ] Official identity, geographic scope, historical start date if shown, and public logo agreed.
- [ ] Each published program has an owner and an accurate status.
- [ ] Ongoing work, completed work, plans, and measured results are clearly distinguished.
- [ ] Any event date/time/place is verified, including timezone where useful.
- [ ] Participation eligibility and relevant supervision arrangements are clear.
- [ ] A public contact route is approved, tested, and monitored.
- [ ] Photo sources and permissions are recorded.
- [ ] Goals are clearly different from achieved results, with a source for each result claim.
- [ ] Demo wording and search metadata reflect the actual release status.
- [ ] A phone user can complete the main information path.
- [ ] Hosting/repository/domain owners and renewal responsibility are known.
- [ ] The technical backup can locate the handover and recovery instructions.

## Maintenance rhythm (proposed)

- **When an activity changes:** its coordinator sends the update promptly; urgent cancellations get a clear visible notice.
- **Before sharing an event link:** verify date, venue, status, and contact again.
- **After an event:** collect a short factual recap and permissioned photos; label outcomes with their source.
- **Monthly:** editor checks stale agenda entries, project ownership, broken links, and contact availability.
- **Quarterly:** maintainer reviews dependencies and tests a fresh build; backup reviews access.
- **Before domain renewal:** the domain owner confirms payment and continued organizational control.

Agree an achievable response expectation; do not promise round-the-clock volunteer support.

## Continuity for a one-technical-person team

Use an organization-owned repository when practical, retain more than one authorized owner, and place domain ownership with the association. Keep recovery credentials in an appropriate private password manager, not in this public repository. The backup should know how to trigger the workflow, identify a failed build, and ask for support.

Keep a copy of the repository and a known-good release outside the primary workstation. Before any on-premises service launches, its owner must also own backups and recovery drills; a server without an operator is not an operational service.
